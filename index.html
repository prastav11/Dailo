03 // INSPIRATION

People Who Changed How I Think

1879 — 1955

Albert Einstein

"If I were not a physicist, I would probably be a musician."

There is something beautiful about that thought. Physics attempts to understand the structure of reality. Music attempts to express something we cannot always put into words. Both can reveal patterns that were invisible before.

STEPHEN HAWKING
"While there's life, there is hope."

Stephen Hawking is one of the people who made the universe feel closer to ordinary minds. Through his books and explanations, ideas about black holes, time and cosmology reached people who might otherwise never have encountered them. What inspires me most is the extraordinary reach of an idea: a mind can travel far beyond the physical boundaries of the person carrying it.

RICHARD P. FEYNMAN

The Beauty of an Ordinary Mind

"I don't know anything, but I do know that everything is interesting if you go into it deeply enough."

Feynman fascinates me for a different reason. He was less interested in appearing extraordinary and more interested in understanding things — deeply, honestly and from first principles.

What I admire is the idea that extraordinary thinking does not necessarily begin with an extraordinary mind. Sometimes it begins with an ordinary mind that refuses to stop asking questions.

Keep asking. Keep thinking. Keep going beyond what is already known.

08 // MUSIC & COSMOS

Everything Begins Somewhere

Every piece of music begins with something remarkably small

A note can be understood through frequency, harmonics and overtones. Look closer and patterns emerge. Look closer still and those patterns can be described through smaller structures, oscillations and relationships.

It makes me wonder about the universe. If we keep looking deeper and deeper — from patterns, to fragments, to smaller structures — how far can we go? At some point we arrive at something unimaginably small. And from that beginning came a universe containing everything we know.

Isn't that fascinating?

Perhaps that is why music and physics fascinate me in completely different — yet strangely connected — ways. Both invite us to look at something beautiful and ask:

"What is it really made of?"
